# Authority incident-report drafting

## When to use this skill

- Use it when the user needs to turn their account of neglect, abuse, or another incident into formal reports for authorities.

- Use it for any formal report, complaint, or statement where facts must be exact and nothing may be guessed.

- This skill is generic report-drafting; it is not tied to any one case, agency, or jurisdiction. Adapt the agencies, statutes, and intake routes to the user's situation.

## Collect facts with explicit blanks, never guesses

- Collect the facts from the user's account and evidence, and mark anything unknown as an explicit `[NEED]` blank.

- Never guess a date, a name, a number, or a detail to fill a blank; the blank stays until the user supplies it.

- Keep a running list of the `[NEED]` blanks so the user can see exactly what is still missing.

## Draft separate reports per agency from one fact base

- Keep one fact base: the collected facts, the timeline, and the evidence list.

- Draft a separate report per agency from that one fact base, shaped to what each agency needs.

- Never let the drafts drift apart; when a fact changes, update the fact base first and then every draft.

## Separate fact from belief in the wording

- Write what happened as fact and what the user believes as belief, and keep the two visibly separate.

- Do not present a belief, an interpretation, or a motive as a fact.

- When a sentence mixes the two, split it.

## Build a timeline from the evidence

- Build the timeline from the user's screenshots, messages, and records, not from memory alone.

- Give each timeline entry a date or time and the evidence it rests on.

- Mark entries whose timing is uncertain as uncertain; do not firm up a shaky date to make the timeline tidy.

## Anonymize on request, and verify the name is gone

- When the user asks for anonymization, remove their name and identifying details from the report drafts.

- After anonymizing, search every draft for the name and confirm it appears nowhere.

- Keep the anonymized drafts separate from any version that carries the name.

## Keep a private record separate from filed reports

- Keep a private incident record — the full account, including anything not for filing — in a separate file from the reports.

- Produce a SEND version of each report with the private record stripped out.

- Never send or share the private record; only the SEND version leaves the user's hands.

## Research the actual statutes before advising

- Research the actual statutes and rules for the user's jurisdiction before advising on anything legal, such as recording law or confidentiality rules.

- Cite the statute by name and number when you rely on it; do not advise from a general impression of the law.

- When the law is unclear, say so and point the user toward free legal aid for their jurisdiction.

## Give evidence-preservation guidance

- Tell the user to keep the unedited original of every piece of evidence.

- Tell them to keep two backups outside the house, in separate places.

- Tell them to attach a context note to each item with timestamps: what it is, when it was made, and what it shows.

## Route to the right intake

- Find the right intake for each agency: the phone line, the online form, or the office that takes the report.

- Give the user a short call script for each intake so they know what to say and what to have ready.

- Include free legal-aid contacts for the jurisdiction alongside the agency intakes.

## Standing rules

- Never guess; a `[NEED]` blank is better than a filled-in guess in a formal report.

- Never claim more than was checked; a draft is a draft until the user reviews and files it.

- Keep the user's private record private; nothing in it goes into a report without their say-so.
