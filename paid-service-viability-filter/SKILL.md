# S72 — Paid-service viability filter: which projects survive as paid online services

Decide which of the user's projects can be rebuilt or shaped into credited
or paid online services — by cutting everything an existing free product
already beats, and keeping only the survivors.

## When to use

- The user asks which of his projects could become paid online services,
  credit-per-use services, or subscriptions.
- The user asks which projects are worth rebuilding for money at all.
- Do NOT use this to price a service or build billing — this skill only
  decides WHAT survives the filter. Mapping the credit unit and the rebuild
  for the pick is the next step, after the user picks one.

## Map the credit or paid fit first

- List every project and sort it by how it would charge: credits per use
  (one job per run — a build, a model, a capture, a grade, a crawl),
  subscription or one-time paid (accounts, seats, entry, cosmetics), or
  not re-closable as paid (already public under an open licence, or a
  desktop installer with no service shape).
- State the credit unit in plain words for each credits-per-use fit, such
  as "credits per app captured and cloned" or "credits per grade and
  polish cycle".
- Name the blocker honestly: none of the projects has billing or credits
  built yet — each one needs a rebuild as web API plus accounts plus a
  credit ledger, and the legal chain (licence, LLC, server, website) has
  to be in place before any of them can take money.

## Check what is already free — his own, then the world's

- First pass: which of his projects are ALREADY free and public (open
  repo, free download, $0 local-first). A paid online version of one of
  these is a rebuild on top of a free desktop tool that already exists —
  say so, do not hide it.
- Second pass — the cut that matters: for each project, name the EXISTING
  free product or service that already does its job better. This is about
  other people's free tools, not his own. If a free product gives better
  results, the project is CUT from the paid shortlist, and the free killer
  is named next to the cut.
- A cut needs a named killer, not a vibe. "Blender is free open source,
  full modeling, sculpting, rigging, animation, and rendering" is a cut;
  "there is probably something free" is not.
- Ask the user which meaning of "free" he means if it is unclear — his
  own projects being free, or the world's free products beating his. The
  two passes give different answers, and answering the wrong one wastes a
  round.

## Keep only the survivors, ranked

- What survives is a project with NO better free version anywhere: a combo
  nobody free does, or a job nobody else is doing at all.
- Rank the survivors: the one with the clearest unique combo first, a
  strong slice of a larger project second (a pipeline slice can survive
  even when the full editor is dead), and an unproven-but-unique one last
  as an explicit wild card — with the "unproven that anyone wants to pay
  for it" caveat stated, not buried.
- Stop at the shortlist. Do not map the credit model or start the rebuild
  until the user picks one — the pick is his call.
