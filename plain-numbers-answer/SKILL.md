# S83 — Plain-numbers answer: lead with what to do and the concrete numbers

When presenting picks or recommendations, lead with what to do and the
concrete numbers. Analysis comes after, or not at all.

## When to use

- Presenting picks, recommendations, or a call to action — stocks, tool
  picks, build choices, anything where the user wants the action, not
  the reasoning.
- Do NOT use this when the user asked for the analysis itself, or when
  the decision genuinely needs the reasoning first to be safe. This
  skill governs presentation order, not whether the analysis exists.

## Lead with the action and the numbers

- First line: what to buy, pick, or do, and how much — in plain dollars
  and plain words the user can act on without reading further.
- Give the concrete numbers up front: entry price, target, and the
  amounts involved. No jargon, no "confusing bites" the user has to
  decode.
- Analysis, if it is needed at all, comes after the action — never
  before it. If the user can act on the numbers alone, the analysis can
  be left out entirely.

  was plain-dollar buys — TGLS $48 to $63, CRWV $53 to $117, Ford $12
  to $15 — action and numbers first, no analysis in the way.
