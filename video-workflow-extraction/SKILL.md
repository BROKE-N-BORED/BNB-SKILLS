# S25 — Video workflow extraction

Watch a video all the way through, extract the actual workflow or lesson, and give an honest critique of what is real, what is hype, and what fits the user's budget and hardware.

## When to use

- The user sends a video and asks what it shows, what to take from it, or whether its claims hold up.

## Watch the whole video, not the caption

- Watch the video all the way through before you summarize anything, because the lesson is often in the second half.

- Pull stills from ALL of the video files when you need to check a detail, instead of trusting captions or a single frame.

- Correct your own misses by going back to the frames, and say what you corrected, when a still shows something your first pass got wrong.

- Cross-check the creator's own thread or post when the video does not show the real prompt text or the real steps, and say when the video leaves the key detail out.

## Extract the actual workflow or lesson

- Write out the workflow as steps, in the order the video shows them, naming what each step actually does.

- Say what the video shows and what it only implies, and do not fill a gap in the video with a guess about what the creator did.

- Name the tools the workflow uses, and flag any tool that costs money or needs hardware the user does not have.

## Give an honest critique

- Say what is real and what is hype, in plain language, such as when gorgeous footage is a video model dreaming on top of a crude blockout and there is no real file behind it.

- Judge what is usable against the hard rules, that everything must be $0 and must run on the user's own PC, and name what breaks those rules.

- Say what the user can take from the video even when the video itself oversells, such as a game-feel lesson or a trick worth trying on its own.

- Do not praise a workflow you would not recommend, and do not soften a hype call to be polite.

## Map the pipeline onto existing machinery, only when asked

- Map the extracted pipeline onto the machinery the user already has, copying the pipeline and not the tools, when the user wants the workflow built.

- Rewrite any prompts short, sectioned, and fill-in-the-blanks, so they work for small local models instead of assuming a frontier model.

- Generalize the scope when the user corrects it, such as taking a games-only pipeline and making it work for any project through profiles, instead of defending the narrower scope.

- Implement the result as a skill and a module under the [isolated-copy build discipline](../isolated-copy-build-merge/SKILL.md), and verify it before it is held for merge.

## Alternatives for better results

- Ask the user which video they meant when the file they sent does not match what they described, instead of analyzing the wrong video.

- Offer to transcribe the audio when the lesson lives in what is said rather than what is shown.
